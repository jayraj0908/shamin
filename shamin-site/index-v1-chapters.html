01Our Story

From a Greyhound bus
to a hospitality empire.

Richmond, Virginia — 1970

In 1970, P.C. Amin left his rural farming village in India and crossed the sea seeking opportunity. With a master's degree in engineering from the University of Utah, he boarded a Greyhound bus and travelled state to state looking for work. With $20 left in his pocket, he stepped off in Richmond, Virginia.

Eight years later, a stay at a friend's hotel changed everything. "I didn't have money," he said, "but I had a dream." With his brother-in-law B.N. Shah, he purchased a bankrupt 104-room inn in Lumberton, North Carolina — the first Shamin hotel.

From that lone roadside inn, Shamin Hotels grew into the largest hotel owner and operator in Virginia — and it remains, at its core, a family company.

"Every associate is equal, everyone's opinion is equal, and everyone works as a team to provide a memorable guest experience." — Neil Amin, Chief Executive Officer
